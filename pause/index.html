Foothold Now

DBT · Distress tolerance

Something just landed hard.

A message, a look, a silence, and now there's heat in your chest and a story forming fast. This is a short, guided pause built on DBT distress-tolerance skills. Five steps, one at a time, before you do anything you can't take back.

Nothing is saved unless you choose to keep a pause at the end, and even then it stays only on this device, in your browser. Nothing is ever sent anywhere or seen by anyone.

Step 1 · Stop

Stop—just for a moment.

You don't have to figure anything out right now. You only have to not act yet. The moment can wait for you. For the length of this pause, hold off on:

  • sending another message
  • apologizing
  • withdrawing
  • confronting them

None of these are off the table forever. They're just not for right now—not until the wave has had a chance to pass.

Step 2 · Name it

Name what's here.

Naming a feeling loosens its grip a little. Tap whatever fits—feelings and urges both. You can pick more than one.

Step 4 · Slow down

Let the wave come down.

Follow the circle. Breathe in as it grows, out as it shrinks. Let your jaw unclench, your shoulders drop, your hands go soft. Feelings peak—then they fall. Give this one the time to fall.

Step 5 · Remember

My feelings are real, but my interpretation hasn't been confirmed yet.

The fear is real. The shame is real. The urge is real. What isn't confirmed is the story—what it meant, what they intended, what happens next. That part is still a guess, and you don't have to act on a guess.

Where you landed

Keep this pause? Saved only on this device, so you can look back later. You can delete it anytime.

From here you get to choose—wait, check the facts, or respond from steadier ground. Whatever you pick now, you're picking it, not being swept into it.

This is a self-guided practice for the heat of a moment—not therapy, and not a crisis service. If you might be in danger or thinking of harming yourself, please reach out to a crisis line or someone you trust right now. In the US you can call or text 988.

Where this comes from

This pause draws on Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), developed by Marsha M. Linehan. It borrows from DBT's distress-tolerance skills—especially the STOP skill (Stop, Take a step back, Observe, Proceed mindfully) and checking the facts: noticing that a feeling is real while the interpretation driving it may not be confirmed.

How this differs from the ladders

Foothold Now is mostly about exposure and response prevention (ERP)—approaching what you avoid, on purpose, over time. That is planned practice. This pause is different: it's a distress-tolerance skill for an unplanned hard moment, to help you get through the wave without doing something you'd take back. The two sit side by side—one builds tolerance for fear on your own schedule, the other steadies you when a moment lands out of nowhere.

Foothold Now is a personal practice companion, not treatment, and nothing here is medical advice.
