Let it drift

When a thought hooks in, you don't have to argue with it or shove it away. Write it out below, then let it drift—a small practice from ACT for loosening your grip and letting a thought pass, the way thoughts do.

If it helps, begin with “I'm having the thought that…”—naming it as a thought is already part of the unhooking.

What am I actually doing here?

Cognitive defusion is a core skill in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT). Fusion is when a thought and reality feel like the same thing—when “I'm going to mess this up” lands as a fact to obey rather than a string of words passing through. Defusion is the unhooking: seeing a thought as a thought, and letting it be there without letting it drive.

This is not about deciding the thought is false, or forcing it to leave. You're not deleting anything. Writing it out puts a little space between you and the words; watching it drift away rehearses something true—that a thought can come and go on its own, without you gripping it or acting on it.

It borrows the spirit of ACT's leaves on a stream exercise: you set each thought on a leaf and let the current carry it, again and again. Defusion and the leaves exercise come from ACT, developed by Steven C. Hayes and colleagues.

How this differs from the ladders. Most of Foothold Now is exposure and response prevention (ERP)—facing what you avoid on purpose so fear can settle. Defusion is a different move: not confronting the fear, but loosening your grip on a thought so it pulls at you less. They sit side by side, and this practice is complementary to the exposure work, not part of it.

Foothold Now is a personal practice companion, not medical care. If a thought is about harming yourself or someone else, please reach out to a crisis line or someone you trust.
