Sample ladders you can start from

Here are three ready-made exposure ladders for common patterns—just-right, perfectionism, and avoidance—each adapted from the ERP literature and rated 0–100. Tap Add on any of them to drop it straight into Foothold Now, then make it yours: reword the steps in your own words and re-rate them for you.

A starting point, not a prescription. These are illustrative examples, not medical advice. Anxiety and OCD are personal—the wording and the ratings that matter are yours. ERP is genuinely easier and safer with a therapist trained in it; if you're working with one, build your ladders together.

1. Just-right

The distressing sense that something is uneven, incomplete, or not quite right—until it's fixed, redone, or lined up. The exposure is to leave things “off” on purpose and resist the urge to correct them, so the feeling can pass on its own.

Why: practice letting things be “off” without fixing them.

Leave a picture frame slightly crooked and walk away25
Put a few books back out of order and leave them35
Send a text without re-arranging the words to feel “right”45
Set objects on my desk deliberately uneven, all day55
Close a drawer or app “halfway,” resisting the urge to redo it65
Write a paragraph and don't re-read or even out the wording75
Leave a task feeling incomplete overnight, no final “just right” check85

Adapted from the research on “not just right experiences” (Coles et al., 2003) and symmetry/ordering exposure in Foa et al. (2012).

2. Perfectionism

When “good enough” never feels good enough, and mistakes feel intolerable—so you re-check, redo, and polish long past the point of use. The exposure is to make small imperfections on purpose and let them stand.

Why: practice “good enough” and tolerating small mistakes.

Send a casual text after reading it only once25
Leave a small typo in a low-stakes message on purpose35
Send an email after a single read, no re-checking45
Hand in a task at “good enough” rather than perfect55
Make a deliberate small slip in front of someone65
Post something publicly without editing it over and over75
Let a visible imperfection stand in finished work I care about85

Adapted from perfectionism exposure work in Antony & Swinson (2009).

3. Avoidance

When anxiety and OCD run mostly on steering around triggers—so the obsession never has to fire and the fear never gets tested. The exposure is to approach what you avoid, on purpose, while holding back the usual outs (washing, checking, reassurance). This example uses a contamination theme; swap in whatever you avoid.

Why: practice approaching what I avoid, without the usual outs.

Touch a doorknob and wait ten minutes before washing30
Touch a light switch, then eat a snack without washing first40
Use a public door handle and delay washing twenty minutes50
Touch the outside of a trash can lid and carry on with my day60
Hold a handrail in public, no hand sanitizer afterward70
Shake hands, then use my phone without washing80
Touch something I think of as “very contaminated,” resist washing an hour90

Adapted from contamination/exposure and ritual-prevention hierarchies in Foa et al. (2012) and Abramowitz (2018).

How to use them

Tapping Add creates the ladder in Foothold Now. From there:

  • Reword every step in your own language—your triggers are specific to you.
  • Re-rate each step 0–100 by how hard it feels, not how hard it “should.” The ladder reorders itself, gentlest first.
  • Delete what doesn't fit and add what does. A ladder that's truly yours is the one that works.
  • Decide your response-prevention rule for each step (the compulsion you'll hold back), and jot it in the step's Notes.

Common questions

What is an OCD exposure ladder?

An exposure ladder (also called a fear ladder or exposure hierarchy) is a list of situations OCD makes you avoid, broken into small steps and ordered easiest to hardest by how much distress each brings, rated 0–100. In ERP you climb it on purpose while resisting the compulsion, so the distress can fall on its own.

How do I use these sample ladders?

Pick the closest one, tap Add, then make it yours: reword each step, drop what doesn't fit, add what does, and re-rate everything 0–100 for you. The samples are a starting point, not a prescription.

What is just-right OCD?

Tied to what researchers call “not just right experiences,” it's the distressing sense that something is incomplete or uneven until it's fixed or redone. Exposure means leaving things off on purpose and resisting the urge to correct them.

Are these ladders a substitute for therapy?

No—they're educational examples adapted from ERP literature, not treatment. ERP works best with a trained therapist, especially for OCD, where subtle mental rituals are easy to miss. If you work with a clinician, build your ladders together.
